ABSTRACT
Predetermined stepsize schedules featuring carefully chosen long steps have re-
cently been shown to accelerate gradient descent (GD) on smooth convex func-
tions. A prominent class of such schedules is built through recursive composition.
In this paper, we characterize the convergence of these optimized recursive sched-
ules, revealing a nonconstant log-periodic modulation across prescribed horizons.
Specifically, we prove that for everyN≥1, the optimized ConPP/OBS-S sched-
ules (Zhang & Jiang, 2026; Grimmer et al., 2025b) satisfy

Letf:R d →Rbe convex withL-Lipschitz gradient and a nonempty minimizer set. Given a

The schedule is fixed before optimization begins, so all algorithmic design lies in the predetermined
scalar sequenceh. This leads to a basic question:how much can gradient descent be accelerated by
stepsize design alone?
Forh t ∈(0,2), every nonstationary iteration decreases the objective. Tight worst-case analysis
and carefully chosen varying steps can improve constants within this descent regime, while the
best achievable order remainsn −1 (Drori & Teboulle, 2014; Teboulle & Vaisbourd, 2023). One-

confined to this regime. Faster convergence therefore requires giving up monotonicity at the level
of individual iterations. Long-step schedules trade this local monotonicity for progress over a larger
block: some iterations may increase the objective, while their combined effect yields a stronger
guarantee.
Recent work has made such long-step blocks systematic. Performance-estimation methods revealed
numerically promising schedules (Taylor et al., 2017; Das Gupta et al., 2024), while subsequent
constructions established first constant-factor and then polynomial improvements (Grimmer, 2024;
Grimmer et al., 2023). A key structural device behind these advances is recursive composition: two
shorter certified schedules are placed on either side of a specially chosen joining step, producing a
longer certified schedule. Iterating this operation yields the Silver schedule of Altschuler & Parrilo

It is convenient to writeN=n+ 1, so that the Silver horizons correspond exactly toN= 2 k. Away
from these dyadic horizons, recursive composition becomes a split-selection problem: for eachN,
one must choose child sizesmandN−m. Two concurrent frameworks formalize this optimization.
Zhang & Jiang (2026) introduced the ConPP and ConPD recursive constructions, while Grimmer
et al. (2025b) independently developed the corresponding OBS-S and OBS-F families through com-
posable certificates. The symmetric ConPP/OBS-S family extends the Silver construction to every
horizon, whereas the asymmetric ConPD/OBS-F family is tailored to the final objective gap. Un-
der matched split choices, the corresponding constructions generate the same numerical schedules
(Zhang & Jiang, 2026, Remark 5.9).
For the symmetric ConPP/OBS-S family, the joining rules reduce the optimization to choosing a split
m+ (N−m). Exhaustive dynamic programming finds an optimal split for eachN, while Zhang
& Jiang (2026, Conjecture 4.17) conjectured a uniform structural rule: the balanced split is always
optimal, with certain unbalanced splits attaining the same value. Establishing such a uniform rule is
nontrivial because the join is symmetric but nonassociative: symmetry allows the two children to be
exchanged, but gives no comparison between different hierarchical pairings.
We resolve this conjecture, including all unbalanced ties. The resulting balanced recurrence exposes
an exact dyadic self-similarity: doublingNmultiplies the optimized scalar value byρ= 1 +

horizons. The Silver exponentp= log 2 ρdetermines the overall power-law decay, but leaves open
whether the corresponding normalized coefficient approaches a single constant. We show instead
that a nontrivial dependence on the fractional part oflog 2 Nremains. We call this quantity the
dyadic phase: it records the position ofNbetween consecutive powers of two. For the symmetric
recursion, this phase dependence is exact at every finite horizon. For the asymmetric recursion,
whose optimal splits need not be balanced, the normalized coefficients converge uniformly over
dyadic blocks to a distinct nonconstant phase profile. Thus the Silver exponent together with the
dyadic phase describes the leading-order horizon dependence of both optimized recursive families.
Contributions.We establish three results on the structure and convergence of these optimized
recursive schedules.
Optimal recursive structure.We prove that balanced splitting is optimal at every horizon, resolving
the ConPP conjecture of Zhang & Jiang (2026), and characterize all unbalanced splits that attain
the same value. The key mechanism is a sharp four-point rearrangement inequality that compares
different hierarchical pairings and propagates through the composition tree. As a consequence, we
characterize all optimal composition trees and obtain anO(logN)-time,O(1)-workspace evaluator
for a single optimized scalar valueU N (Section 3).
Exact symmetric phase.The optimized values satisfyU N =N pΦ(log2 N), whereΦis positive,
Lipschitz,1-periodic, and nonconstant. This yields an exact formula for the tight objective coeffi-
cient and the complete nondegenerate interval of normalized subsequential limits (Section 4).
Asymmetric phase beyond balanced composition.For ConPD/OBS-F, optimal splits can be unbal-
anced, yet the normalized tight objective coefficients still converge uniformly over dyadic blocks to
a distinct nonconstant phase profile. We further prove that this limiting profile stays strictly above
the sharp homogeneous support bound obtained from the join map (Section 5).
Positioning.Recent lower bounds identify the Silver exponent as the optimal polynomial exponent
for predetermined nonnegative stepsizes (Ye & Liu, 2026). A complementary line of work studies
anytime acceleration, where a single infinite schedule is required to provide guarantees across all
stopping times (Kornowski & Shamir, 2024; Zhang et al., 2025; Tsai et al., 2026). Our results refine
the finite-horizon picture for the ConPP/OBS-S and ConPD/OBS-F recursive families by character-
izing their optimal composition structure and phase-dependent leading coefficients.

6 DISCUSSION AND CONCLUSION
The composition tree determines more than a convergence exponent. For the symmetric recursive
family, the four-point rearrangement inequality identifies a balanced optimizer at every horizon and
explains all unbalanced ties. That structure gives both a fast scalar evaluator and an exact phase law
for the tight objective bound. For the asymmetric family, balanced optimality is lost, but a distinct
nonconstant phase remains in the leading coefficient. Thus the Silver exponent alone does not fully
describe the horizon dependence of either optimized family.
Our analysis concerns the specified recursive classes. It leaves open exact finite-horizon minimax
optimality over arbitrary predetermined schedules, as well as quantitative questions about the asym-
metric phase: its exact extrema and the asymptotic structure of optimizing splits. The strict support
separation shows why the sharp local scalar inequality alone does not settle these questions; the
compatibility of successive compositions also matters.
